Privacy policy

Last updated 2026-10-28.Draft: not yet reviewed by a lawyer.Applies to the Kyslap closed-test version for Android.

In short

  • Kyslap works without an account. If you choose to sign in with Google, you can write questions and share answers, and some of what you share is visible to other readers (see “What other readers can see”).
  • This version has no analytics. It may offer a one-time supporter purchase through Google Play (see “Supporter purchase, through Google Play”); Google handles the payment, and we never see your payment details. It can show ads from Google AdMob, only after you finish your daily goal: one ad on the Done screen, and a video you choose to watch for a bonus round. Ads may be switched off for everyone; when they are on, Google receives device and ad data described in “Ads, through Google AdMob”, and where the law requires it you are asked first.
  • Your interests, goal, and reading history stay on your phone, kept separately for each account you sign in with there, with one exception: to find questions other readers wrote about articles you have read, the app sends the ids of some of those articles to our database, with no name or account unless you are signed in (see “Questions from other readers”).
  • The app asks Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons for the articles and images it shows. That tells them your IP address and what was requested.
  • The app sends crash reports to Sentry only if you say yes. It asks once when you first set the app up, and you can change your answer in Settings.
  • We do not sell your data. Kyslap itself does not track you across other apps or websites; Google’s ad service may use your advertising ID, as described below.

1. Who is responsible

Kyslap is made and published by an individual developer based in the Philippines (“we”). The developer is the person responsible for your data and the person to contact about it: kyslap.app@gmail.com. The developer is also the accountable person for data protection; there is no separate data protection officer.

The law we have most in mind is the Philippines’ Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act 10173). We have also written this policy to be clear to people in other countries who may use the app, such as in the European Union, the United Kingdom, and the United States.

2. What stays on your phone

Kyslap keeps a database in the app’s private storage on your phone. We never receive a copy of it.

One history per account. If you sign in with Google, the app keeps a separate history for each account signed in on this phone, and one for when nobody is signed in. The first account you sign in with on the phone takes over the history you had before; any other account starts with its own; signing out shows the signed-out history. To open the right one, the app keeps, next to each history’s file name, the ID our database gives that account (not its name or email). Your crash-report choice, the note of a supporter purchase, the last ads and purchase switch file, and the Wikipedia answers kept to avoid asking again belong to the phone, so every history shares them.

Each history holds:

  • Your choices: the topics you picked (and, as a list, every topic you have ever picked, even if you later switched it off), including any topic you typed in yourself, saved as the title of the Wikipedia article you confirmed; your daily goal, whether streaks are on, and a reminder time (saved only; this version sends no notifications). (The crash-report switch belongs to the phone, as above.)
  • What you have seen and done: which articles were shown to you (and which version), the articles you saved (their titles, the topic each was shown for, and a copy of the bite, so the Saved list can reopen it offline), and a skip list of articles the app could not turn into a bite. For each bite: how long you stayed, whether you swiped sideways, saved it, opened the full article, or marked it “not interested”. Also a log of what the app chose for you and why (the topic group and the chance it was picked), and your daily progress (how many bites you finished, and how many bonus rounds you took). For each bonus round: the day, its size, and how many bites you had read when it started, kept for 7 days so the next round’s size can follow how many bonus bites you actually read. If you buy the supporter purchase, the app keeps a note on your phone that you did (the product and the time), and, while a payment is pending, a note that it is. It also keeps the titles and topics of the bites you finished today and your one-tap answer on whether the goal felt too short, right, or too long. For the knowledge tree on the Progress tab, it keeps each article you finish, including those you open with “Go deeper”: its title, the topic it was picked for, when you first finished it, whether you read it or only swiped past, and a copy of the bite (its text and image credits), so you can reopen it offline. These stay until you clear the app’s data or uninstall it.
  • Bites ready to read: the next few bites (their text and image credits), fetched ahead so they work offline, and today’s bites, kept for up to 3 days so they survive closing the app.
  • Your dives: when you tap “Go deeper” and open a related article, the app keeps that dive on your phone for up to 3 days, and you can reopen it only on the day you made it. It holds the articles you opened (their titles and text, with image credits), the path between them, how long you stayed on each, whether you saved one or opened it in full, and which of them counted toward your daily goal. Nothing about a dive is sent to us.
  • Wikipedia answers kept to avoid asking again: search results (including those for topics you typed) for 6 hours. Article text and image details are kept only while the app is open. The app’s image loader may also keep the photos you have seen on your phone.
  • Your recall questions: for each question you answered in a recall check, which question and article it was about, how many bites earlier you read the article, and whether you answered it or used “I don’t remember”. It holds no score and not the words you typed.
  • Your reviews: for each sentence you answered a recall question about, the app keeps its article title, the question last asked about it (written by another reader, with their display name), when the sentence is next due for a review, how you last answered, and a log of each review (when, how well it was remembered, and whether it was a typed or a multiple-choice question). It also keeps which articles you read have a question written about them, so the Progress tab can show how many do. A review looks for another question about the same article by sending the article’s id to our database, as in section 3. The words you typed are not kept, and none of this is sent to us.
  • People you blocked and questions you hid: if you block someone or hide a question from the “⋯” menu or an answer, the app keeps their ID (and, for a person, the display name they had when you blocked them) on your phone, so it can leave them out and show you the list. Blocking and hiding need no account. The IDs are sent to our database with your requests for questions and for other readers’ answers (see section 3), which is how they are left out.
  • Your sign-in, if you sign in: a session token, kept in the phone’s encrypted secure storage, so you stay signed in. Signing out or deleting your account removes it from the phone.
  • A request counter: the times of recent requests to Wikipedia and an hourly limit the app has learned for you, so it stays within Wikimedia’s rules. It holds no article titles or search terms.
  • The short clip pack, if you download it: short wordless video clips and a list of them, kept in the app’s private storage, and when each clip was last shown (so a clip does not repeat for 3 days). The Delete button in Settings removes all of it.

The app uses these only to choose what to show you next and which question to ask you. Nothing in this list is shared with anyone, except that, when the app looks for a question about an article you have read, it sends that article’s id (see “Questions from other readers” in section 3), and an answer you choose to share is sent as described there.

3. What is sent over the network

To Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons

These are run by the Wikimedia Foundation. To build a bite, the app requests article excerpts and image details, and your phone then loads the images. Like any web request, this reveals your device’s IP address and the request itself to Wikimedia: for example the topic it is searching for, an article title, the links in the part of an article you were reading when you tapped “Go deeper” and a short description of each article they point to, the words you type when you add your own topic (sent as a search so you can confirm the article), an image file name, or the request for Wikipedia’s featured article of the day. Each request carries a User-Agent that names the app, its version, and this website, as Wikimedia’s API policy asks; it contains no personal identifier. Wikimedia handles that data under its own privacy policy.

Questions from other readers: Supabase

Readers can write short questions about the articles they read. From your fifth bite on, Kyslap looks for one about an article you have already read; if none is found it looks again at each following bite until one is, and then again five bites later. To do that the app asks our database, which is hosted by Supabase (see below), and sends the ids of up to 50 articles you have read, the ids of the questions this phone has asked you, and the ids of any authors you have blocked on this phone, so they are left out. This happens whether or not you are signed in. If you are not signed in, no name, email address or Kyslap account goes with it (if you have reported something, the background account that report created goes with it); like any web request it still reveals your IP address to Supabase, whose server logs keep it for a short time (see below). If you are signed in, the request carries your sign-in, so questions you have already answered are skipped and questions by people you follow come first. The question comes back with its author’s display name and avatar; you answer it on your phone first, and nothing you type leaves the phone unless you sign in and agree to share your answers: once you have agreed, your later answers are shared as you give them, and you can delete any of them (see “What other readers can see”). If you follow an author, we store that you follow them; your list of people you follow is private, and the author sees only how many people follow them.

If you sign in or report something: Google and Supabase

Signing in is optional; the app works without it. If you sign in, Google’s sign-in screen (part of Google Play services on your phone) asks you to choose a Google account and gives Kyslap’s database an ID token for it. Our database is hosted by Supabase (Supabase Pte. Ltd., in Singapore, on Amazon Web Services). If you sign in, Supabase stores your Google account’s email address and ID, the name and profile-picture link Google sends with them (we never show these to other readers), your display name and avatar, and, as you use the social features, your questions, votes, follows, reports and answers (the option you chose or the words you typed) with their times, plus a short note that you answered a question (without the answer) so it is not asked again. We do not ask your age or birth date. Kyslap is for people 13 and older. If you are under 18 and unsure whether to sign in, ask a parent or guardian.

When the app asks our database for questions to show you, or for other readers’ answers to a question, it sends the IDs of the people you blocked and of the questions you hid, so those are left out. A report sends what you reported (a question, an answer or a person) and the reason you picked.

If you report something while you are not signed in, the app first creates an anonymous account in the background so the report can be sent. That account has no profile, name or email address; it holds only the reports it sent.

Supabase keeps short-lived server logs of requests, which include IP addresses and, for sign-in events, the email address and name of the Google account, for 24 hours. Its sign-in service also keeps a record of each signed-in session, which includes an IP address, while you stay signed in; the record goes when you sign out or delete your account. The page for deleting your account on this website loads Google’s sign-in script from Google, so Google sees that visit; the page sets no cookies of its own. If the Google account you sign in with has no Kyslap account, the sign-in briefly creates an empty one, which the page deletes at once.

When you open an article or a license

“Read the full article”, in the “Go deeper” menu, opens the Wikipedia page in a browser tab shown inside Kyslap. That tab is run by your phone’s own browser, so your browser’s cookies, sign-ins, and settings apply there, and the app cannot see what you do in it. Links to licenses and to this website open your phone’s browser directly. Those sites handle your data under their own policies.

Crash reports, to Sentry

Kyslap sends crash reports to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.) so we can fix bugs. A report contains the error message, the app’s code trace at the moment of the crash, the app version, whether it is a test or a release build, basic device details such as the Android version and phone model, and a random ID that the crash tool creates for the installation, so we can tell how many phones hit the same bug. That ID is not linked to your name or account, and it may change if you reinstall the app.

We do not send the app’s database, and reports do not include your name or email address. The app removes web addresses and article titles from error messages before sending them. We turned off screenshots, session replay, performance tracing, and usage tracking, and the app removes network, navigation, and console notes from reports. We do not send your IP address on purpose and have asked Sentry not to store it (Sentry’s servers still see it when the report arrives). Sentry handles the reports under its own privacy policy.

Crash reports are off unless you choose “Yes” when the app first asks, or turn on “Send crash reports” in Settings. If you say no, nothing is sent. They are sent only from release builds. You can switch them off again in Settings: errors stop being sent at once, and the native crash handler stops after you restart the app.

If you download the clip pack: GitHub

Only on Wi-Fi, when you open the clip pack settings or tap Download, the app fetches a list and the clip files from the release downloads of this website’s repository at GitHub (github.com/Eureka401/eureka401.github.io). Like any web request it reveals your IP address and the request to GitHub (a company in the United States). Nothing about you is sent with it, and the clips are never streamed.

Whether ads are on: GitHub Pages

At most once each time it runs, when you reach the Done screen or open Settings, the app reads a small settings file from this website (remote-config.json, hosted by GitHub Pages) that says whether ads and the supporter purchase are switched on. Like any web request it reveals your IP address to GitHub. Nothing about you is sent with it. The app keeps the last answer on your phone; if it has never read the file, ads stay off.

Ads, through Google AdMob

When ads are switched on, the app uses Google’s Mobile Ads SDK (Google LLC). Nothing about ads happens before you finish your daily goal. On the Done screen the app first runs Google’s consent tool (the User Messaging Platform): if you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, or in a US state with its own privacy law, it shows Google’s message and asks for your choice, and you can say no as easily as yes. Only after that does the app start the ad SDK and ask for ads: one ad on the Done screen, marked “Ad”, and, if you tap “Bonus round”, one video ad. Closing that video early costs nothing; you just don’t get the extra bites.

When the app asks for an ad, Google receives your phone’s advertising ID (unless you have deleted it or turned off ad personalization in your phone’s settings), your IP address, basic device and app details (such as the phone model, Android version, language, and the app’s name and version), your consent choice, and what happens with the ad (whether it was shown, watched to the end, or tapped). Google uses this to choose and measure ads and to prevent fraud, and, if you allow it, to personalize them. Google handles it under its own privacy policy and explains how it uses data from apps that use its services. Kyslap sends Google nothing about what you read, your topics, your questions, or your account.

You can change your ad choices at any time in Settings under “Ad privacy choices” (shown where Google’s consent tool requires it), and reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone’s settings (on most phones: Settings → Privacy → Ads, or Settings → Google → Ads).

No tracking by Kyslap

Kyslap itself does not track you across other apps or websites and has no analytics tools. The only tracking-related data is what Google’s ad service receives, described above, which your consent choice and your phone’s ad settings control.

Supporter purchase, through Google Play

When the supporter purchase is switched on, Settings shows it with Google Play’s price. Buying it, or tapping “Restore purchase”, opens Google Play Billing (Google LLC) on your phone: Google handles the payment under its own terms and privacy policy, and we never receive your card, bank or payment details, your name or your email. Each time it runs, and when you tap “Restore purchase”, the app asks Google Play on your phone which Kyslap purchases your Google account holds (so a supporter keeps the purchase after a reinstall), and it confirms each paid purchase to Google Play. It sends nothing about you to us: whether you are a supporter is kept only on your phone, so after a reinstall “Restore purchase” (or the next start) asks Google Play again. Supporters see no ads, so the ad steps in “Ads, through Google AdMob” do not run for them.

4. What other readers can see

When you answer a question, the person who wrote it and the other readers who answered it can see your answer with your display name, your avatar and your badge. Nobody can reply to an answer or message you. Your display name starts as “Reader 1234”, and we never show your Google name or email address. Please don’t use your real name. If you use a nickname or details people already know you by, such as one from your school or another app, they may recognise you from it. Nothing is shared until you sign in with Google, agree to the terms, and answer: your answer is first checked on your phone, and “I don’t remember” is never sent. The app shows you this notice before your first shared answer: “Your answer is shared with your display name and avatar: the question’s author and other people who answered can see it. You can delete it at any time, and it is deleted with your account.” You can delete any answer (the “Delete my answer” button under the question), or your whole account, whenever you like. We keep a note that you answered the question, without the answer, so it is not asked again. We cannot remove copies someone makes by screenshot. Other readers can report an answer, and we may hide or remove it.

If you sign in and write a question, we store it with your account, together with the sentence you quoted and the article it came from. It is published under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 license beside your display name, as the terms explain, and other readers can report it. Before anyone sees it, our server checks that the quoted sentence is in the version of the Wikipedia article you read: it asks Wikipedia for that version by its page and revision numbers, which carry nothing about you. Your first question waits for our review. You can delete a question at any time; deleting your account deletes all your questions and the answers to them.

Anyone using the app can open your profile, which shows your display name, avatar, badge, reputation and how many people follow you. It does not show who you follow, who follows you, or how you voted. Your votes on questions are not shown to anyone; they only count towards the author’s reputation and the order questions are shown in.

5. Why we use data, and on what basis

  • To run the app and choose bites for you. You asked the app to do this when you installed it and picked topics. The data stays on your phone. Nothing is needed from you beyond using the app.
  • To get articles and images from Wikimedia. The app cannot show you content without asking for it.
  • To give you the features you asked for. If you sign in, we use your account data for your account, your questions and answers, and for showing them to others as described in “What other readers can see”. Signing in is optional; the app works without it.
  • To show ads after your goal, which pay for the app. Where the law requires consent (the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland), ads are personalized only if you agree in Google’s message; otherwise, and elsewhere, they rest on our interest in funding a free app, limited to the Done screen. You can change your choice in Settings (“Ad privacy choices”) and object by resetting or deleting your advertising ID.
  • To fix bugs with crash reports. This rests on your consent, and the data is small and limited. Crash reports are optional: you give it by choosing “Yes” when the app asks or by turning them on in Settings, and you withdraw it by switching them off in Settings, which takes effect as described above.

We do not make decisions about you by computer that have legal or similarly important effects. The app’s choice of the next bite is a content suggestion that happens on your phone.

Sentry

GitHub

Google (AdMob)
When ads are switched on and only after your daily goal: the device and ad data described in “Ads, through Google AdMob”, including your advertising ID. Google uses it as an independent controller under its own terms, and as our processor where Google’s terms say so.
Google (Google Play)
If you buy or restore the supporter purchase: the purchase itself, handled in Google Play under your Google account and Google’s terms, as described in “Supporter purchase, through Google Play”. We receive no payment details.
Supabase and Google
Supabase hosts our database (in Singapore, on Amazon Web Services, with support access from the United States), and Google provides the sign-in. They process the data for us, if you sign in or report, and Supabase also receives the article ids described under “Questions from other readers” whenever the app looks for a question. Other readers see only what “What other readers can see” lists.
Your phone’s browser
Whatever you open from a link (an article, a license, this website).

Wikimedia receives these requests as an independent service, and Sentry, Supabase and Google process data for us under their own terms. We do not sell your personal information. When ads are on, Google AdMob receives the data described in “Ads, through Google AdMob” to show and measure ads; in some US states that kind of sharing can count as “sharing for advertising”, and Google’s message there lets you opt out. We have no analytics tools.

7. Transfers to other countries

We are in the Philippines, but Wikimedia, Sentry, Supabase and Google run their services elsewhere. Crash reports go to a Sentry address in the United States. Requests to Wikipedia go to Wikimedia’s servers, which may be outside the Philippines. By using the app you send those requests from your phone to them. We remain responsible for the crash reports we send, and they are sent only if you say yes; you can turn them off in Settings.

If you sign in, our database is in Singapore, support access can come from the United States, and its encrypted weekly backup is stored at GitHub in the United States. We remain responsible for your data when it is processed for us abroad, and we use Supabase’s data processing terms for it.

8. How long data is kept, and deleting it

  • On your phone: your database stays until you delete it. Clearing the app’s storage (Android Settings, Apps, Kyslap, Storage, Clear data) or uninstalling the app removes all of it, every account’s history included. Deleting your account in the app also deletes that account’s history on that phone. A few items expire by themselves, as listed above (today’s bites and your dives after 3 days, saved search results after 6 hours).
  • Crash reports at Sentry: Sentry deletes them automatically after its retention period. Because the install ID is random and cannot be read inside the app, we cannot look up your reports by name. If you want one removed, email us the date it happened, your phone model, and the app version, and we will do what we can.
  • At Wikimedia: see Wikimedia’s privacy policy, linked above.
  • Your account and what you shared: answers and questions stay until you delete them or your account. You can delete your account in the app (Settings, Account) or on our website, at the delete-account page. Deleting it also deletes your questions, your answers, your votes and follows, and your reports. Deleting it in the app also deletes its reading history on that phone; deleting it on the website cannot reach your phone, so that history stays there until you clear the app’s storage. Deleted data leaves our encrypted backups (stored at GitHub) within 28 days. Short-lived server logs, which include IP addresses and, for sign-in events, the Google account’s email address and name, are kept for 24 hours. Reports and moderation records are kept without your name.
  • If you never sign in, there is no account to close.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to:

  • be informed about how your data is used (this policy);
  • access your data and correct it;
  • object to its use, or withdraw any consent you gave (for crash reports, switch off “Send crash reports” in Settings);
  • have it erased or blocked;
  • receive a copy of it;
  • claim damages if it is misused; and
  • complain to a data protection authority: in the Philippines, the National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph); elsewhere, the authority where you live (you can complain to the National Privacy Commission of the Philippines or to your local data protection authority).

Most of your data is on your phone, where you control it: you can see your choices in the app, change them in Settings, and delete everything as described above. If you have an account, you can change your display name and delete your answers or your whole account in the app. For anything else we might hold (your account data, crash reports), email kyslap.app@gmail.com. We will answer as soon as we can. We may ask for the details in section 8 to find a report, and we will not ask you for more personal information than that.

10. Children and teens

Kyslap is for people 13 and older. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. The app does not ask your age, so we cannot check it. We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13. Parents who find that a child under 13 has signed in can email us to have the account deleted. Teens can use the social features; what they share is shown as described in “What other readers can see”.

If you are under 18 and unsure whether to sign in, ask a parent or guardian. We have tried to write this policy so that a teenager can read it.

11. Security and breaches

Your database is kept in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read on an unmodified phone. The app contacts Wikimedia and Sentry only over encrypted (HTTPS) connections. Without an account we hold almost no personal data, so there is little to lose. Account data is sent to Supabase only over encrypted (HTTPS) connections. If a breach puts your data at risk, we will tell the National Privacy Commission and the people affected within 72 hours, in plain language.

12. Ads, purchases, and notifications

Ads are described in “Ads, through Google AdMob” in section 3. There are no ads before your daily goal, none between bites, and none when the app opens or closes. Purchases: the one-time supporter purchase is described in “Supporter purchase, through Google Play” in section 3. It is for life: it never expires, and it covers every paid feature the app adds later. Notifications: the app saves a reminder time you can choose, but it does not send notifications.
